DiaLLM: An Investigation into the Robustness-Generation Gap in English Dialect Adaptation

DiaLLM is a study investigating the robustness-generation gap in adapting large language models to dialectal English. The research introduces DiaLLM, which pretrains three open-weight language model families and applies various post-training paradigms combined with alignment strategies for Australian, Indian, and Northern British English. The findings highlight that while benchmarks shape model robustness through continual pretraining and fine-tuning, explicit variety-targeted adaptation produces more dialectally recognizable output than broad alignment methods, though not always judged as such by human evaluators. This work underscores the need for richer reward designs and continued investment in dialectal resources to bridge the gap between model performance and human perception.

Google has implemented a new anti-scraping measure by rewriting organic search result links to use `google.com/goto?url=` instead of exposing destination URLs directly in the HTML.
