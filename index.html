How vanilla's history is not so … 'vanilla'

Vanilla's history is complex and intertwined with colonialism, slavery, and scientific ingenuity. The crop's global cultivation began in Mesoamerica but faced challenges due to the lack of a natural pollinator until an enslaved 12-year-old boy named Edmond Albius discovered the manual pollination method on Reunion Island in 1841. This breakthrough made vanilla cultivation possible on a large scale, particularly in Madagascar, which became the world's largest producer. However, despite his crucial contribution, Edmond never profited from his discovery and died in poverty. The widespread use of synthetic vanillin later diminished vanilla's perceived exclusivity and complexity, overshadowing its rich historical significance.
